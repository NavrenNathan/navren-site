How it runs

Flows first, then everything else.

The automated sequences do the heavy lifting, so they get built before anyone worries about the weekly send.

Weeks 1–3

The core flows

Welcome and post-purchase first, because they touch every customer. Then cart and browse abandonment, which are the ones with money visibly falling out of them.

Weeks 3–6

Segmentation and reviews

Splitting the list by real behavior, and building the review request sequence. Both quiet, both compounding.

Ongoing

Campaigns and testing

Regular sending against a calendar, subject line testing, and pruning the parts of the list that have stopped listening.

Why it's first for commerce

It pays back in weeks, not months.

Almost everything else in marketing takes a quarter before you know if it worked. Flows run against traffic you already have, so the answer arrives quickly, which makes it the sensible place to start when trust still needs building.

It's also the only channel you truly own. Platforms change their rules and their prices; a list doesn't.

Straight answers

What people ask about email.

Our list is small. Is it worth it?
Usually yes, because flows work per-person rather than per-send. A welcome sequence to two hundred engaged people can outperform a newsletter to five thousand who've forgotten who you are.
How often should we send?
More than you'd guess, less than most agencies push. It depends on what you sell and how often people buy. The real limit is whether each send is worth opening.
Will this annoy people?
Only if it's irrelevant. Unsubscribes climb when you send the same thing to everyone regardless of what they've done, which is what segmentation exists to prevent.
