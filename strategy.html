How it runs

Two to four weeks, then it's yours.

Strategy work that takes a quarter has usually stopped being strategy.

Week 1

Listening

Your customers, your sales conversations, your competitors, your numbers. Most of what we need already exists inside your business, it's just never been written down in one place.

Week 2–3

Deciding

The positioning, the offer, the channel order and the budget. Decisions, with the reasoning attached, so you can disagree with the logic rather than just the conclusion.

Week 4

Handing over

A document you keep and an hour walking through it together, so the reasoning lands as clearly as the conclusions.

When it's worth it

Before you spend, not after.

The most expensive version of this work is the one done after six months of underperforming ads, because by then you've paid for the strategy twice: once in media, once in fees.

If you're about to launch, about to raise spend, or about to hire your first marketer, that's the moment this pays for itself several times over.

Straight answers

What people ask about strategy.

Isn't this just a document nobody reads?
It is, if it's written to impress you. We write it to be used, the language goes straight into your site, your ads and your sales calls. If it can't be used that way, it wasn't strategy, it was a deck.
What's a fractional CMO actually do?
Sets the direction, decides the channel priorities, and holds whoever's executing to account. It suits companies spending enough that mistakes are expensive, but not enough to justify a six-figure hire.
Can we just skip to the advertising?
Sometimes that's the right call, and if your positioning is already proven we'll go straight to the channel work. More often the message hasn't been tested properly, and advertising an untested one is the expensive way to find out. A week or two up front usually pays for itself in the first month of spend.
